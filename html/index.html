2. How much to trust this

  • Verified means "exists". For each video the YouTube oEmbed lookup returned the same video ID, and titles and channels are copied exactly as returned. Nobody watched the videos. "What you will get" comes from titles, chapter lists and the channel's usual format.
  • Lengths are mostly unknown. YouTube returned a bot-protection page for most watch pages and the agents did not work around it. Per the track files: A has a confirmed length for 8 of 47 main videos (2 h 58 m), B for 11 of 48 (about 4 h 13 m, plus lower bounds for two Karpathy talks), C for 28 of about 50 (at least 25.6 h combined). No total can be stated yet. Opening each link once fills the "length ?" cells.
  • The planning below uses a guess. I assumed about 30 minutes per video, so two videos per ride. Lectures run longer (Karpathy's Deep Dive into LLMs is at least 3 h 21 m on its own), so expect to get through fewer videos than the plan says. Re-plan after week one.
  • Level and Mode tags are judgement calls, not lookups.
  • Tools change fast. In Track C, anything published before 2025 is flagged "may be dated" in its table, and even 2025 material on frameworks and services can be a release or two behind by now.

3. The routine (bike mode)

  1. Ride: two videos, in table order, from the block you are on. Watch the intuition video before the lecture; the tables are already ordered that way.
  2. Right after: answer that block's recap questions out loud, then open the hidden answers. Missed one? Re-watch only that video. Do not push on.
  3. One line of notes: "what hurt, and what fixed it" for each video. If you keep this habit, the Atlas style of thinking carries over directly.
  4. Desk items are optional. Never let a DESK video eat a ride. Save them for a weekend session, if at all. Track E is the desk path: one block per weekend session, after the Track D block it pairs with.
  5. If you fall behind, cut in this order: optional lists first, then Track A Block 7 (discrete math), then Track B Blocks 7-8, then Track E labs you have not reached, then the later Track C blocks. Never cut the first three blocks of Track C, or Track A Blocks 1-6: the rest builds on them.
  6. Keep the roadmap out of your main YouTube history. Watch on a separate Chrome profile or a second Google or brand account signed in on the bike monitor. Playlists for the Atlas step should be created on that account too. Adding to a playlist does not touch watch history; playing does.

Track F is the full course tracker. It has one block for each of the 14 courses on the cdso page (not just the four foundational ones above), with free public materials (textbooks, course sites) and a prep path per course. It opens with a ranking of which electives sit closest to the GenAI-architect goal. The ranking is my judgement from the course outlines, not salary or hiring data. In short: Deep Learning, Advances in Deep Learning, Natural Language Processing and Advances in Deep Generative Models are the closest; Machine Learning, Optimization, Reinforcement Learning, Ethics in AI and Case Studies are useful; Planning, Online Learning, Automated Logical Reasoning, AI in Healthcare and AI in Astrophysics are niche for this goal. Track F also lists four questions for the program coordinator (which courses run when, weekly hours, whether the four foundational courses are mandatory, and what Advances in Deep Learning covers). The cdso page shows no course numbers or syllabi, so confirm the mapping in the UT handbook or with the coordinator before choosing a semester plan.
